# Research

Background on Complex PTSD (C-PTSD) and the grounding and self-regulation techniques that were used to design each activity in Tending.

## What is C-PTSD?

Complex Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder is a mental health condition that results from prolonged and repeated trauma that is seen in survivors of childhood abuse, domestic violence, or captivity, where the traumatic situation feels impossible to escape.

A C-PTSD diagnosis starts with a licensed professional assessing the core symptoms of PTSD:

- Re-experiencing trauma through nightmares, flashbacks, or intrusive memories.
- Avoidance of internal and external reminders of the trauma.
- A persistent sense of threat, resulting in startle reflexes and hypervigilance.

Symptoms of C-PTSD were originally studied in the context of childhood trauma, but prolonged exposure to traumatic events during adulthood is also strongly linked to it. A person can experience trauma without developing PTSD or C-PTSD. As with other mental health conditions, it becomes a clinical concern when it impacts daily living and the ability to navigate life.

Cloitre et al. (2011) describe three recommended phases of treatment:

1. Stabilization, resourcing, and self-regulation
2. Processing of traumatic memories
3. Consolidation of treatment gains

Tending is designed to support the first phase, stabilization and self-regulation, not the later phases, which require a trained therapist.

## Techniques that inspired the activities in Tending

- **Grounding**: Splashing water on the face, describing surroundings out loud, counting objects and naming colors, naming things you can smell and touch, moving specific parts of the body.
- **Body awareness**: Going through the body region by region to notice tension, numbness, or pain (progressive muscle relaxation and body scanning).
- **Paced breathing**: A well-established technique for downregulating the nervous system's fight or flight response.
- **Self check-in**: Noticing and naming physical andemotional states (hunger, thirst, temperature, tension, loneliness, overstimulation) before they build into distress.
- **Self-compassion ("Self Friend")**: Asking what you would say to a friend in the same situation, and caring for one's child-self (e.g., Dr. Kristin Neff).
- **Knowing your triggers**: Sounds, places, words, dates, media, or people that reliably signal distress, so they can be recognized rather than mistaken for present danger.
- **Supportive relationships**: Validation, active listening, fostering connection, authenticity, and presence, though these fall outside what a solo app can provide directly.
