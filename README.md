# c-ptsd-self-therapy

Games

1. Self Check-In
   Quick tap-through of icons (hungry, thirsty, hot/cold, tired, tense, lonely, overstimulated). Low art lift — this can be simple flat icons, doesn't need a full scene.
2. Body Relaxation (progressive muscle relaxation)
   A simple humanoid silhouette; prompts guide the player through tense-then-release by muscle group, highlighting each region as it's addressed. Medium lift — one asset (the silhouette figure) reused across states via color/glow changes rather than needing new art per step.
3. Body Check-In (body scan)
   Same silhouette base as #2, different mode — player taps a region and reflects on how it feels (color-codes it: tense, numb, fine, painful). You could actually build #2 and #3 as two modes of one underlying scene/asset, which saves real dev time.
4. Orientation
   A simple environment (window, calendar, clock) where the player answers "what day/season/where am I" prompts. Low art lift — a couple of static background elements do the job.
5. Paced Breathing
   A shape that expands/contracts on a timer (or reactive to mic amplitude, tying back to your voice idea). This is the cheapest one visually — a single shader/particle effect can carry the whole activity, no real "art" needed at all.

Feel of Gris

- **Depression** — the core clinical tool is behavioral activation (small concrete actions counteract withdrawal/low motivation). This is _very_ game-native — a garden/village that grows through tiny daily quests ("make your bed," "step outside," "text one person") is basically gamified behavioral activation, and it's a much better fit for quest-and-reward framing than trauma work is.
- **ADHD / task initiation** — body-doubling, timers, "just start for 5 minutes" — this maps naturally to a companion-creature or co-op mechanic, low stakes, momentum-based.
- **Grief** — something quieter and narrative, closer to your Gris tone than to a quest system — a memory-garden or scrapbook-world you slowly build and revisit, letting the world change over time the way grief does.
- **Chronic pain / pacing** — actually fits your hackathon's "physical health" angle directly — a game that teaches activity pacing (the real clinical skill for chronic pain/fatigue conditions) by having the player manage an energy resource without over-spending it.
- **Social anxiety** — low-stakes rehearsal scenarios, practicing a hard conversation with an NPC before doing it for real — this is close to actual exposure-hierarchy therapy, gamified.
- **Anger/emotional regulation** — a "cooldown" mechanic literally, pairing a breathing or pause mechanic with in-game stakes so the pause has a reason to matter.

Care for your child-self

A CPTSD diagnosis starts with a licensed expert assessing if the person is experiencing core symptoms of PTSD:

Presently re-experiencing their trauma with nightmares, flashbacks or intrusive memories.
Avoids internal and external reminders of their trauma.
Feels a persistent sense of threat, resulting in startle reflexes and hypervigilance.

Symptoms of complex PTSD were originally focused on childhood trauma. However, prolonged exposure to traumatic events during adulthood is strongly linked to CPTSD. Individuals can also understand complex PTSD by comparing it to other mental health challenges like depression. Everyone experiences stress, sadness or depression to some degree. However, when levels of these emotions come to impact daily living and hinder the ability to navigate life, that is when they become clinical diagnoses, such as Major Depressive Disorder. That said, a person can experience trauma and not develop PTSD or CPTSD.
